Hi, I'm Frederick. I am currently a research associate in the Department of Comprehensive Dentistry at the University of Texas Health Science Center at San Antonio.

My research is balanced between experimental and computational work for materials science. At the bench, I design and test polymeric composites that are formulated with antimicrobials to reduce instances of secondary caries. Computationally, my work focuses on automating the extraction of legacy data from literature to generate feature-rich datasets, aiding materials discovery within the broader scientific community.

A historical challenge is addressing the trial and error nature of material development process. Simply put, it is prohibitively expensive and slow, thus forming the rationale and principle guide behind my interest in the methods used in their design. This encompasses techniques such as fine and coarse-grained modeling, data-driven inference, and autonomous approaches.

I studied Biomedical Engineering and Chemistry at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill and North Carolina State University. There, I was previously an Abrams Scholar conducting bioinformatics research at UNC's Chemical Research Instrumentation Teaching and Core Labs, evaluating representative lipids as standardized features to quantify lipidome integrity.

Broad topics of interest: materials characterization, informatics, autonomous experimentation, and open problems in discovery/design.
